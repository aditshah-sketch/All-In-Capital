## What it does
Aerolekh builds a fixed-wing VTOL drone with 90 minutes of endurance and a 3 kg payload, aimed at surveying and mapping. The airframe and flight controller are designed in-house; motors and batteries are imported. They have built 4 prototypes and flown a public demo over a 600-acre plot near Hosur, producing a 3 cm resolution map. They hold a DGCA type certificate application but it is not yet filed in full. There is no paying customer and no pilot agreement. They are raising ₹5 Cr to build 20 units and get type certification.

## Founder assessment
Arjun Mehra (aerospace engineering from a top IIT, 6 years at a defence research lab) and Vikrant Sood (ex avionics engineer at a large aircraft manufacturer, then MBA from a top business school). The deck leads with logos of their past employers and colleges. The demo was impressive and Arjun answers technical questions in depth. When Aditya asked who would buy the first 20 units, the answer was "state governments, mining companies and agri insurers," with no named contact in any of them. There was no milestone plan beyond "certification in about a year." Asked about unit cost, Vikrant gave ₹14 L but could not break it down.

## Risks and open questions
Survey drones are a crowded category with at least 15 Indian makers and price pressure from government tenders. Certification timeline is undefined and the full application is not filed. No customer conversation means price, required features and sales cycle are all guesses. Government buyers pay in 120 to 180 days, which strains working capital. ₹5 Cr for 20 units implies very high cost per unit until volumes grow. Founders have not sold anything before. Imported motors add currency risk.

## Why we passed
Strong credentials and a good demo, but no customer, no named buyer and no milestone plan. Once we took the logos away there was very little evidence to score. We score evidence, not pedigree. Aditya said he would take a second call if they come back with one paid pilot and a dated certification plan. Riya's first draft score was higher than it should have been because of the past employers on the deck, and we corrected it in the partner meeting. The founders were gracious about the feedback, which we noted.
