## What it does
Mitti Credit lends working capital to small dairy farmers and farmer producer organisations in Banaskantha and Mehsana. Loans are ₹30,000 to ₹1.5 L for cattle feed and a second animal, repaid from milk payments the cooperative society deducts at source. They lend from their own NBFC licence application track, currently through a co-lending setup where Mitti carries 20% first-loss on its own book. Book size is ₹3.2 Cr across 1,140 borrowers. 90-day overdue is 1.9%. They are raising ₹4 Cr, of which ₹2.5 Cr goes into the loan book and ₹1.5 Cr into team and the licence.

## Founder assessment
Pradeep Chaudhary (8 years in rural branch banking, last role as area credit manager) and Meenal Joshi (data, ex analytics at a microfinance lender). Before lending a rupee they spent 13 months in 46 villages, visiting borrowers with the milk society secretaries, building a scorecard on milk yield, number of animals and society payment history. Pradeep has personally sat in on 300+ loan assessments. When asked about defaults Meenal pulled up the vintage curve by village and explained the two villages where overdue went to 6% (a fodder shortage in April). They have rejected 38% of applicants so far.

## Risks and open questions
Concentration: 70% of the book sits with three milk cooperatives, so a dispute with one society could freeze deductions. First-loss of 20% means a bad season hits their equity directly. The NBFC licence could take 12 to 18 months, and until then they depend on one co-lending partner. Monsoon failure or a cattle disease outbreak would raise overdue across the whole book at once. Team is 11 people and field cost per loan is ₹1,100, which needs to fall as ticket size stays small.

## Why we invested
The founders own the hardest risk in the business, which is credit. They did the field work first, they carry first-loss, and they can explain every bad village. That is the opposite of founders who treat defaults as someone else's problem. Overdue under 2% on a 14-month-old rural book with evidence behind the scorecard was enough for us. We invested ₹1.5 Cr. Aditya was sceptical on the licence timeline and asked for quarterly updates on it. Field cost per loan is the number we will watch most closely.
