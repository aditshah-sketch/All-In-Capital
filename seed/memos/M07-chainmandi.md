## What it does
Chainmandi is a rupee to stablecoin conversion app for freelancers and small exporters who get paid by overseas clients. A freelancer receives USD stablecoins into a Chainmandi wallet and converts to rupees into a bank account, or holds the balance. Fee is 0.8% per conversion. They have 2,300 sign-ups from a waitlist and ran a closed beta with 140 users moving ₹1.1 Cr over 3 months. The founders' plan assumes that 1% TDS on crypto transfers is withdrawn and that clearer rules on stablecoin use for export payments arrive within 18 months. They are raising ₹2 Cr.

## Founder assessment
Ishaan Bhatia (ex backend engineer at a crypto exchange, 4 years) and Neha Gupta (compliance, 3 years at a payments aggregator). Both understand the product and the user pain well; Neha has mapped FEMA reporting for export receipts in detail. Ishaan built the beta in 10 weeks. When asked what happens if the TDS stays, Ishaan said conversion volume would be "a fraction" because users lose 1% on every transfer and also face 30% tax on gains, and that the model "really only opens up" once the rules change. They had not modelled a version of the business under current rules.

## Risks and open questions
The core economics depend on a tax and regulatory change that has not been signalled by the government. Banks often refuse or freeze accounts linked to crypto exchanges, which affects payouts. Export receipts through stablecoins may not count as valid foreign exchange inflow for GST export benefits. Enforcement attention on crypto platforms is high. If the rules do change, larger exchanges with existing user bases could add this feature quickly. Beta users were mostly the founders' own network. Wallet custody adds security cost.

## Why we passed
We do not back companies whose existence depends on a favourable regulatory change. Chainmandi's founders were honest about it, which we appreciated, but the business does not work under today's rules and they have no plan B. Crypto is also outside our stated thesis. Pass, with a note that we would like to hear from Neha on future projects. A business that improves if rules change is fine; one that only exists if they change is not something a pre-seed fund can underwrite.
